## 01 // Purpose & Principles

KittyLM is an independent artificial intelligence and language model research project dedicated to rigorous scientific experimentation, reproducible engineering, transparent evaluation and responsible technological development.

Our community brings together interests in machine learning, neural architectures, Python development, cybersecurity, data engineering, high-performance computing and scientific research.

We recognise that meaningful technical progress depends on more than working code. It requires intellectual honesty, constructive criticism, respect for others, careful experimentation and a willingness to acknowledge mistakes.

This Code of Conduct establishes the behavioural, professional and ethical standards expected of everyone participating in KittyLM.

Our guiding principles are:

- **Scientific Integrity:** Represent research methods, evidence and results accurately.
- **Engineering Excellence:** Develop reliable, maintainable, secure and reproducible software.
- **Respectful Collaboration:** Encourage thoughtful discussion, inclusive participation and constructive disagreement.
- **Responsible Innovation:** Consider the security, safety, privacy and wider implications of AI research.
- **Transparency & Accountability:** Acknowledge limitations, disclose relevant conflicts and accept responsibility for contributions.
- **Continuous Learning:** Support curiosity, experimentation, constructive feedback and professional development.

> **Our philosophy:** Build it. Measure it. Understand it. Improve it.
>
> **Our community standard:** Challenge ideas with evidence. Treat people with respect.

## 02 // Our Pledge

As project maintainers, researchers, contributors and community participants, we pledge to make participation in KittyLM a welcoming, respectful and harassment-free experience.

This commitment applies regardless of:

- Age or level of experience
- Body size or physical appearance
- Visible or invisible disability
- Ethnicity, nationality or race
- Gender identity, gender expression or sex characteristics
- Sexual orientation or identity
- Religion or personal beliefs
- Education, professional background or academic qualifications
- Socio-economic circumstances
- Programming, engineering or research experience

We recognise that valuable scientific contributions can come from students, independent researchers, experienced engineers, hobbyists and people entering the field for the first time.

Technical expertise does not confer permission to demean, intimidate or exclude others.

Every participant deserves to be treated with dignity.

We pledge to foster an environment where individuals can ask questions, contribute ideas, present evidence, challenge technical assumptions and learn without fear of harassment or humiliation.

## 03 // Standards of Professional Conduct

### 3.1 Expected Behaviour

Examples of behaviour that contributes positively to the KittyLM community include:

- Treating contributors with empathy, patience and professional courtesy.
- Welcoming thoughtful questions and different levels of technical experience.
- Respectfully considering differing opinions, interpretations and engineering approaches.
- Providing clear, specific and actionable feedback.
- Explaining technical disagreements using evidence and reproducible reasoning.
- Giving appropriate credit for research, code, documentation and intellectual contributions.
- Acknowledging mistakes and correcting them when discovered.
- Helping others understand complex technical concepts without condescension.
- Respecting agreed project procedures, contribution guidelines and maintainers' decisions.
- Considering the security, safety and privacy consequences of proposed changes.
- Prioritising the project's long-term integrity over personal recognition or competition.

### 3.2 Unacceptable Behaviour

The following conduct is prohibited:

- Harassment, bullying, intimidation or threats.
- Discriminatory remarks, slurs or exclusionary conduct.
- Unwelcome sexual attention, advances or sexualised harassment.
- Personal attacks, insults or deliberately humiliating criticism.
- Trolling, deliberate disruption or repeated bad-faith engagement.
- Hostile behaviour directed at someone because of their background, qualifications or experience.
- Publishing another person's private information without explicit permission.
- Stalking, targeted harassment or encouraging others to harass someone.
- Retaliation against individuals who raise concerns or report misconduct.
- Deliberately sabotaging project work, reviews or collaborative processes.
- Misrepresenting another contributor's work, statements or research findings.
- Repeatedly disregarding reasonable moderation or safety instructions.
- Other behaviour reasonably considered abusive, threatening or seriously unprofessional in a scientific or engineering environment.

Disagreement about technical decisions, scientific claims, project priorities or research methodology is not inherently a Code of Conduct violation.

**Criticism of ideas is welcome. Personal abuse is not.**

## 04 // Scientific Integrity & Research Ethics

Scientific integrity is a foundational requirement of KittyLM.

All research contributions should be honest, traceable and reproducible to the extent reasonably possible.

### 4.1 Research Honesty

Contributors must not knowingly:

- Fabricate, falsify or selectively misrepresent experimental results.
- Present hypothetical measurements as actual observations.
- Claim that tests, benchmarks or experiments passed when they were not performed.
- Misrepresent training progress, model performance or deployment readiness.
- Conceal known failures or materially relevant experimental limitations.
- Alter research evidence to support a predetermined conclusion.
- Present another researcher's work as their own.
- Misrepresent the provenance of datasets, models, code or evaluation results.

Contributors are expected to distinguish between:

**Verified:** Supported by reproducible evidence or directly inspected results.

**Reported:** Communicated by a contributor but not independently verified.

**Inferred:** A conclusion drawn from available evidence, with stated assumptions.

**Proposed:** A design, hypothesis or future experiment that has not been implemented or demonstrated.

**Unknown:** Information for which sufficient evidence is unavailable.

These distinctions protect the credibility of KittyLM's research.

### 4.2 Reproducibility & Transparency

Where relevant, research submissions should document:

- Experimental objectives and hypotheses.
- Model architecture and configuration.
- Dataset origin and version.
- Tokenizer configuration.
- Training methodology and random seeds.
- Hardware and software environment.
- Evaluation procedures and metrics.
- Known limitations and sources of uncertainty.
- Relevant code revisions, checkpoints and validation evidence.

Contributors must not knowingly omit information that would materially change the interpretation of a research result.

Negative results, failed hypotheses and unsuccessful experiments are legitimate scientific outcomes.

**An honest failed experiment is more valuable than a misleading successful result.**

### 4.3 Corrections & Scientific Disagreements

Research findings may be challenged, corrected or superseded as new evidence becomes available.

Contributors should respond to good-faith scientific criticism professionally.

Honest errors should ordinarily be handled through correction, documentation and improved verification.

Deliberate fabrication, repeated deception or intentional suppression of material evidence may warrant stronger action.

Disputed scientific findings should be assessed using evidence and the project's research governance processes. An ordinary technical disagreement should not automatically become a conduct complaint.

## 05 // Engineering Collaboration & Code Review

KittyLM values engineering quality, clear communication and maintainable software.

All contributors participating in code reviews, pull requests, technical discussions and development planning are expected to maintain professional standards.

### 5.1 Constructive Technical Review

When reviewing code or architecture proposals:

- Focus on correctness, maintainability, security and measurable behaviour.
- Explain concerns clearly and reference relevant evidence.
- Distinguish critical defects from stylistic preferences.
- Recommend practical improvements where possible.
- Avoid dismissive or humiliating language.
- Give contributors reasonable opportunities to explain their approach.
- Acknowledge valid corrections, regardless of who identifies them.
- Respect the difference between verified defects and speculative risks.

For example:

**Constructive:** "The checkpoint loader appears to lack validation for this metadata field. Could we add a regression test?"

**Unacceptable:** "You clearly have no idea how checkpointing works."

Technical review is intended to strengthen the project, not undermine the people developing it.

### 5.2 Engineering Accountability

Contributors should follow approved development procedures, including applicable testing, CI, documentation and security requirements.

Knowingly submitting fabricated test evidence, bypassing required safeguards without authorisation, or deliberately concealing significant defects is unacceptable.

Accidental regressions, incomplete implementations and unsuccessful debugging attempts are not misconduct by themselves.

The expected response to engineering mistakes is transparency, correction and appropriate documentation.

### 5.3 Respect for Project Governance

KittyLM is an independently owned and human-directed research project.

Final decisions regarding architecture, research scope, repository management, training, publication and releases remain with the Project Owner or explicitly authorised maintainers.

Contributors may respectfully challenge decisions and propose alternatives.

However, participation does not grant permission to bypass established approval procedures, modify unrelated systems, publish restricted material or undertake unauthorised project operations.

Governance authority must be exercised fairly and must not be used to justify harassment or retaliation.

## 06 // Responsible AI & Data Research

KittyLM research involves language models, training data, experimental artifacts and potentially sensitive computing systems.

Community members are expected to approach these resources responsibly.

### 6.1 Data Provenance & Intellectual Property

Contributors should:

- Respect dataset licences and applicable usage restrictions.
- Identify the origins of externally sourced research material.
- Attribute third-party code, models and research appropriately.
- Avoid submitting private or confidential information without authorisation.
- Respect the intellectual property and licensing rights of others.
- Disclose relevant dataset limitations, restrictions or provenance uncertainties.

Knowingly introducing stolen, unlawfully obtained or unauthorised private material into KittyLM is prohibited.

### 6.2 Security & Privacy

Contributors must not:

- Intentionally expose credentials, access tokens or private keys.
- Publish private datasets or confidential research artifacts without permission.
- Attempt unauthorised access to contributor accounts or project infrastructure.
- Introduce malicious code or intentionally compromise development systems.
- Exploit security weaknesses against project participants.
- Use project infrastructure for unauthorised surveillance or data collection.

Potential security vulnerabilities should be disclosed through the project's [Security Policy](SECURITY.md), rather than through public discussion when disclosure could increase risk.

### 6.3 Responsible Model Development

Research contributors should communicate model capabilities and limitations accurately.

They must not knowingly exaggerate model reliability, conceal material safety concerns or present experimental systems as validated production solutions.

Research into model behaviour, failure modes and security risks is welcome when performed responsibly and within authorised project boundaries.

## 07 // AI-Assisted Contributions

KittyLM welcomes responsible use of AI-assisted development and research tools.

AI systems may assist with coding, documentation, debugging, architecture analysis, testing and experimental planning.

However, AI-generated material does not automatically constitute verified research or reliable engineering.

Contributors remain accountable for the work they submit.

Expected standards include:

- Reviewing AI-generated code before submission.
- Testing generated implementations where appropriate.
- Verifying technical claims and references.
- Disclosing material AI assistance when required by contribution procedures.
- Avoiding fabricated citations, benchmarks and research evidence.
- Respecting applicable licences and third-party rights.
- Ensuring AI-assisted contributions satisfy the same standards as human-written work.

**AI assistance does not replace scientific verification or contributor responsibility.**

## 08 // Scope & Applicability

This Code of Conduct applies to official KittyLM community spaces and project activities, including:

- GitHub repositories, issues and pull requests.
- Code reviews and technical discussions.
- Research documentation and experimental submissions.
- Official project communication channels.
- Collaborative development sessions.
- Project-managed community forums.
- Authorised research events and meetings.
- Public activities where someone is officially representing KittyLM.

It also applies when a person acts as an authorised representative of KittyLM outside its usual community spaces.

Private disagreements unrelated to the project are not automatically within scope. However, conduct outside official channels may be considered when it creates a credible threat to participants or directly affects the safety and integrity of the project community.

## 09 // Reporting Misconduct

If you experience or witness behaviour that may violate this Code of Conduct, please contact the KittyLM project maintainers.

**Community conduct contact:**

`contact@krazykitty.dev`

Reports should, where possible, include:

- A description of the incident.
- The date or approximate time.
- The relevant project location or communication channel.
- Links or supporting evidence, where available.
- Any immediate safety or privacy concerns.

Providing every detail is not a requirement for submitting a report.

Please avoid sharing unnecessary sensitive personal information.

### 9.1 Confidentiality

Reports will be handled with discretion and shared only where reasonably necessary to review the concern, protect participants or implement corrective action.

Absolute confidentiality or anonymity cannot be guaranteed in every circumstance.

Maintainers will seek to minimise unnecessary disclosure of identities, sensitive details and supporting evidence.

### 9.2 Protection Against Retaliation

Retaliation against someone for making a good-faith report, assisting with an investigation or raising a legitimate concern is prohibited.

Knowingly submitting fabricated allegations or using the reporting process to harass others is also unacceptable.

A report that cannot be substantiated is not, by itself, evidence of bad faith.

### 9.3 Security Reports

Security vulnerabilities, credential exposure and other technical security concerns should be reported according to [SECURITY.md](SECURITY.md).

Community conduct reporting is not a substitute for the project's vulnerability disclosure process.

## 10 // Enforcement Responsibilities

KittyLM project maintainers are responsible for interpreting and enforcing this Code of Conduct.

Their responsibilities include:

- Reviewing reports fairly and without unnecessary delay.
- Considering relevant evidence and surrounding circumstances.
- Distinguishing misconduct from legitimate technical disagreement.
- Protecting participants from avoidable harm.
- Applying proportionate corrective measures.
- Documenting significant moderation decisions appropriately.
- Avoiding unnecessary disclosure of sensitive information.
- Identifying conflicts of interest that could compromise impartial review.

Maintainers may remove, edit or reject contributions, comments, issues, pull requests and other material that violate project standards.

They may also restrict participation where necessary to protect the community.

Where reasonably appropriate, affected contributors will receive an explanation of the action taken.

Enforcement decisions should not be based solely on personal disagreements, competing research preferences or criticism of project leadership.

## 11 // Enforcement Guidelines

KittyLM uses a proportionate enforcement framework adapted from the Contributor Covenant's Community Impact Guidelines.

The measures below provide general guidance rather than an automatic sequence. Serious misconduct may justify stronger immediate action.

### Level 1 — Correction

**Community Impact**

An isolated instance of inappropriate, disrespectful or unprofessional behaviour with limited impact.

**Possible Response**

- Private clarification of expected behaviour.
- A request to correct or remove inappropriate content.
- A written reminder of community standards.
- Guidance on preventing recurrence.

The objective is education, correction and constructive resolution.

### Level 2 — Formal Warning

**Community Impact**

A more substantial violation, repeated inappropriate behaviour or conduct that negatively affects other participants.

**Possible Response**

- A formal written warning.
- Clearly stated behavioural expectations.
- Restrictions on particular interactions.
- Notice of potential further consequences.

Additional action may follow if the behaviour continues.

### Level 3 — Temporary Restriction or Ban

**Community Impact**

Serious misconduct, sustained harassment, significant disruption or repeated violations following warnings.

**Possible Response**

- Temporary removal of contribution privileges.
- Suspension from project communication spaces.
- Time-limited participation restrictions.
- Restrictions on contacting affected participants.

The duration and conditions will be determined according to the circumstances.

### Level 4 — Permanent Ban

**Community Impact**

Severe misconduct, repeated serious violations, persistent harassment, credible threats or deliberate behaviour that substantially endangers participants or project integrity.

**Possible Response**

- Permanent removal from official community spaces.
- Revocation of contribution or collaboration privileges.
- Blocking from project-managed communication channels.
- Other reasonable protective measures within maintainers' authority.

Permanent exclusion should be supported by documented reasons and a proportionate assessment of the risk and impact.

## 12 // Fair Process, Review & Appeals

Enforcement should be fair, consistent and proportionate.

Where feasible and appropriate:

1. Reports will be assessed against the applicable Code of Conduct provisions.
2. Relevant evidence and contextual information will be considered.
3. The person involved may be given an opportunity to respond.
4. Conflicts of interest will be identified and considered.
5. Decisions will account for severity, impact, intent where supported by evidence, and repeated behaviour.
6. Corrective action will be documented appropriately.
7. Requests for reconsideration will be reviewed where practicable.

A participant wishing to request reconsideration may contact:

`contact@krazykitty.dev`

A request for reconsideration should explain the disputed decision and provide any relevant additional context or evidence.

Because KittyLM is an independent project, a fully separate appeals panel may not always be available. Maintainers should be transparent about such limitations and seek impartial input where reasonably practicable.

Immediate protective measures may be taken before a full review when necessary to address credible safety or security risks.

## 13 // Research Community Commitments

We want KittyLM to remain a place where careful research, engineering curiosity and respectful disagreement can coexist.

The following commitments guide the community:

**Evidence over ego.**  
Research claims should stand on their supporting evidence, not the seniority or reputation of their authors.

**Learning over humiliation.**  
Asking questions and making honest mistakes are normal parts of engineering and scientific development.

**Accountability over appearances.**  
Acknowledging a defect, limitation or failed experiment is better than hiding it.

**Collaboration over hostility.**  
Different approaches can be discussed rigorously without hostility towards their proponents.

**Responsible progress over artificial milestones.**  
Technical integrity should not be sacrificed to create an appearance of progress.

**Transparency over unsupported certainty.**  
When something has not been verified, we should say so.

These commitments apply to project leadership as well as contributors.
