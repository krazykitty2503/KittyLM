## 01 // Purpose & Principles

KittyLM is an independent artificial intelligence and language model research project dedicated to rigorous scientific experimentation, reproducible engineering, transparent evaluation and responsible technological development.

Our community brings together interests in machine learning, neural architectures, Python development, cybersecurity, data engineering, high-performance computing and scientific research.

We recognise that meaningful technical progress depends on more than working code. It requires intellectual honesty, constructive criticism, respect for others, careful experimentation and a willingness to acknowledge mistakes.

This Code of Conduct establishes the behavioural, professional and ethical standards expected of everyone participating in KittyLM.

Our guiding principles are:

- **Scientific Integrity:** Represent research methods, evidence and results accurately.
- **Engineering Excellence:** Develop reliable, maintainable, secure and reproducible software.
- **Respectful Collaboration:** Encourage thoughtful discussion, inclusive participation and constructive disagreement.
- **Responsible Innovation:** Consider the security, safety, privacy and wider implications of AI research.
- **Transparency & Accountability:** Acknowledge limitations, disclose relevant conflicts and accept responsibility for contributions.
- **Continuous Learning:** Support curiosity, experimentation, constructive feedback and professional development.

> **Our philosophy:** Build it. Measure it. Understand it. Improve it.
>
> **Our community standard:** Challenge ideas with evidence. Treat people with respect.

## 02 // Our Pledge

As project maintainers, researchers, contributors and community participants, we pledge to make participation in KittyLM a welcoming, respectful and harassment-free experience.

This commitment applies regardless of:

- Age or level of experience
- Body size or physical appearance
- Visible or invisible disability
- Ethnicity, nationality or race
- Gender identity, gender expression or sex characteristics
- Sexual orientation or identity
- Religion or personal beliefs
- Education, professional background or academic qualifications
- Socio-economic circumstances
- Programming, engineering or research experience

We recognise that valuable scientific contributions can come from students, independent researchers, experienced engineers, hobbyists and people entering the field for the first time.

Technical expertise does not confer permission to demean, intimidate or exclude others.

Every participant deserves to be treated with dignity.

We pledge to foster an environment where individuals can ask questions, contribute ideas, present evidence, challenge technical assumptions and learn without fear of harassment or humiliation.

## 03 // Standards of Professional Conduct

### 3.1 Expected Behaviour

Examples of behaviour that contributes positively to the KittyLM community include:

- Treating contributors with empathy, patience and professional courtesy.
- Welcoming thoughtful questions and different levels of technical experience.
- Respectfully considering differing opinions, interpretations and engineering approaches.
- Providing clear, specific and actionable feedback.
- Explaining technical disagreements using evidence and reproducible reasoning.
- Giving appropriate credit for research, code, documentation and intellectual contributions.
- Acknowledging mistakes and correcting them when discovered.
- Helping others understand complex technical concepts without condescension.
- Respecting agreed project procedures, contribution guidelines and maintainers' decisions.
- Considering the security, safety and privacy consequences of proposed changes.
- Prioritising the project's long-term integrity over personal recognition or competition.

### 3.2 Unacceptable Behaviour

The following conduct is prohibited:

- Harassment, bullying, intimidation or threats.
- Discriminatory remarks, slurs or exclusionary conduct.
- Unwelcome sexual attention, advances or sexualised harassment.
- Personal attacks, insults or deliberately humiliating criticism.
- Trolling, deliberate disruption or repeated bad-faith engagement.
- Hostile behaviour directed at someone because of their background, qualifications or experience.
- Publishing another person's private information without explicit permission.
- Stalking, targeted harassment or encouraging others to harass someone.
- Retaliation against individuals who raise concerns or report misconduct.
- Deliberately sabotaging project work, reviews or collaborative processes.
- Misrepresenting another contributor's work, statements or research findings.
- Repeatedly disregarding reasonable moderation or safety instructions.
- Other behaviour reasonably considered abusive, threatening or seriously unprofessional in a scientific or engineering environment.

Disagreement about technical decisions, scientific claims, project priorities or research methodology is not inherently a Code of Conduct violation.

**Criticism of ideas is welcome. Personal abuse is not.**

## 04 // Scientific Integrity & Research Ethics

Scientific integrity is a foundational requirement of KittyLM.

All research contributions should be honest, traceable and reproducible to the extent reasonably possible.

### 4.1 Research Honesty

Contributors must not knowingly:

- Fabricate, falsify or selectively misrepresent experimental results.
- Present hypothetical measurements as actual observations.
- Claim that tests, benchmarks or experiments passed when they were not performed.
- Misrepresent training progress, model performance or deployment readiness.
- Conceal known failures or materially relevant experimental limitations.
- Alter research evidence to support a predetermined conclusion.
- Present another researcher's work as their own.
- Misrepresent the provenance of datasets, models, code or evaluation results.

Contributors are expected to distinguish between:

**Verified:** Supported by reproducible evidence or directly inspected results.

**Reported:** Communicated by a contributor but not independently verified.

**Inferred:** A conclusion drawn from available evidence, with stated assumptions.

**Proposed:** A design, hypothesis or future experiment that has not been implemented or demonstrated.

**Unknown:** Information for which sufficient evidence is unavailable.

These distinctions protect the credibility of KittyLM's research.

### 4.2 Reproducibility & Transparency

Where relevant, research submissions should document:

- Experimental objectives and hypotheses.
- Model architecture and configuration.
- Dataset origin and version.
- Tokenizer configuration.
- Training methodology and random seeds.
- Hardware and software environment.
- Evaluation procedures and metrics.
- Known limitations and sources of uncertainty.
- Relevant code revisions, checkpoints and validation evidence.

Contributors must not knowingly omit information that would materially change the interpretation of a research result.

Negative results, failed hypotheses and unsuccessful experiments are legitimate scientific outcomes.

**An honest failed experiment is more valuable than a misleading successful result.**

Contributors should respond to good-faith scientific criticism professionally.
